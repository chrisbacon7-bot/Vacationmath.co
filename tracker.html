Trip expense tracker — common questions

What is a trip expense tracker?

It is where you log what a vacation costs against the plan. This page does that in the browser: categories, a receipt log, a chart, CSV, and a link you can send. Load a sample or start a blank trip. The spreadsheet further down is optional.

What is a vacation budget tracker?

The same tool, aimed at the trip budget rather than a household budget. Planned and actual sit side by side for lodging, flights, food, tickets, transport, insurance, tips, and extras. A 10% buffer is the ceiling unless you change it.

What is a trip budget tracker?

It compares what you planned to spend with what you actually spent. This page does that in the browser by category, with a multi-day log, a chart, and a CSV export. The Excel workbook further down is optional.

How do I track planned vs actual vacation cost?

Enter planned and actual for lodging, flights, food, tickets, transport, insurance, tips, and extras. The result is under plan, inside the buffer, or over the ceiling. Day-log lines are the receipt trail. They do not replace the category actuals until you edit those numbers.

Can I track a vacation that is not Orlando?

Yes. Load a Caribbean cruise, Hawaii, Europe, a Cancún all-inclusive, or a Smoky Mountains drive. Or start blank. Samples link to Trip Plan or the page that published the cost band. Before the trip, turn the total into a monthly target with the vacation savings calculator.

Do I have to download the spreadsheet?

No. Copy a trip link or export CSV from the result. The .xlsx is the same idea for Excel, Numbers, or Google Sheets, with no email gate.

What's the over-count buffer?

A buffer, 10% by default, sits on top of the planned total. The ceiling is plan plus that buffer. Change the percent on this page. In the workbook the same idea is cell D5 on the Plan sheet.

What it does

  • Adds a configurable buffer (default 10%) on top of your plan — biased toward over-counting so you don't get surprised
  • Calculates per-traveler costs everywhere — change the traveler count once, every per-person number updates
  • SUMIFs the Trip Log into Dashboard categories automatically — log a $42 expense, dashboard updates instantly
  • Conditional formatting on the dashboard: red text + pink fill for over budget, green for comfortable, plain for in-between
  • Dropdowns on Category and Split columns so a typo can't break the math
  • Tracks who paid (Card A, Card B, Cash, Other — rename to match your actual cards) for easy reimbursement math
  • Personal vs split expenses — set the Split column per row, per-person share adjusts automatically
  • 50 expense rows ready, easy to add more without breaking formulas
  • All formatting, dropdowns, and the dashboard chart preserved across Excel, Numbers, Google Sheets, and LibreOffice

Workbook questions

Does the file work in Numbers and Google Sheets?

Yes. Numbers opens the .xlsx natively. In Google Drive, open it with Sheets. Formulas, the dashboard chart, and dropdowns come across. You do not need the file to use the tracker on this page.

How is this different from the calculators?

Disney, cruise, all-inclusive, and Trip Plan estimate a trip from 2026 prices. This tracker is where you put that plan next to what you actually spent, including days that were not in the estimate. Use the “compare” links on the result to jump back with the destination filled in.

How we built it

Every default number in the Plan sheet matches the same sources we use for the calculators: official 2026 Disney pricing, NerdWallet, AAA, Touring Plans, and cruise line tariffs (updated September 2026). The buffer is deliberate — we'd rather you over-budget and have money left than under-budget and panic. All formulas use named ranges so they survive when you add or rename rows.
