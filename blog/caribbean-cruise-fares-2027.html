Caribbean cruise fares are down. What would four of you pay?

January fares are about 12% lower than last year, but the tip line alone adds $518 for four on a seven-night cruise.

By Chris Bacon  ·  Founder, Vacation Math  ·  7 min read

Caribbean cruise fares are falling for early 2027, and the Black Friday sales are about to start, so I wanted to figure out what a week at sea really costs a family of four. The fare drop is real, but the number on the ad is only the start of the bill, and I think it’s worth seeing the whole picture before a sale price makes the decision for you.

What fares are doing

The Financial Times reported on October 7, citing Cruise Critic, and Parade picked it up on October 11, that the average advertised starting fare for a five-to-seven-night January Caribbean cruise is $748 a person, down from $846 a year ago. That’s $98 less, or about 12%, and part of the reason appears to be more ships sailing in the Caribbean. Goldman Sachs estimates Caribbean capacity is up about 11.5% year over year. These are average starting prices for a particular group of January sailings, so they don’t mean every cruise is cheaper.

That same Parade piece cited Barclays Research on first-quarter 2027 Caribbean fares. Norwegian was down 21%, Carnival 7%, and Royal Caribbean 8% compared with the same point a year earlier. Those are market-level comparisons and not guaranteed discounts on any one booking. They tell me the drop isn’t even across cruise lines, so it pays to compare more than one.

For a second view, I looked at Fathom Fares, which tracks thousands of sailings and includes taxes and fees in its prices. On October 31 it showed a median interior cabin of $100 per person per night for January 2027, based on 323 sailings, and $118 for March.

What a week adds up to

Here’s an example. Take seven nights for four people at those median rates. That’s $2,800 in January and $3,304 in March, so waiting until spring costs about $504 more. Those fares are per person at double occupancy, so a third or fourth guest in a cabin may be priced differently.

Then add tips. Royal Caribbean charges $18.50 per guest, per day for a standard stateroom, which comes to $518 for four people over seven nights (4 × 7 × $18.50). Other cruise lines have their own gratuity rates, so check yours.

Drinks, Wi-Fi, and excursions are on top of that, and so is getting to the port. A cheap fare can easily become an average one once those lines are added. If a drink package is part of your plan, I worked through that decision in my September break-even post.

Black Friday or wave season?

Royal Caribbean says its Black Friday savings run from November 27 through Travel Tuesday, December 1, and wave season follows in January through March. Cruise lines tend to run similar kinds of deals both times, so I wouldn’t assume one is dramatically better than the other.

What I’d do is price the same ship, the same date, and the same cabin before the sale starts and again during it. Then I’d judge the sale by the total, not by the headline discount. If you want a rule for telling a real deal from a banner, I wrote one in my Black Friday post.

01
Write down today’s price first. Pick the ship, date, and cabin you want, and record the price before any sale begins. That’s your baseline.
02
Compare the all-in total. Add tips, drinks, Wi-Fi, excursions, and travel to the port, then compare the totals instead of the discounts.
03
Check what “per person” means. Fares assume two people in a cabin, so a third or fourth guest can change the math.
