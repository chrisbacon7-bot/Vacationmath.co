84% of Summer Travelers Will Put This Trip on a Credit Card

NerdWallet's 2026 Summer Travel Report just landed, and the debt part of vacation planning isn't going away. Here's the real risk, and the two tools that keep you out of it.

By Chris Bacon  ·  Founder, Vacation Math  ·  6 min read

NerdWallet's 2026 Summer Travel Report just landed, and the debt part of vacation planning isn't going away. Here's what the real risk is, and the two tools that keep you out of it.

The numbers behind the risk

01
Most travelers will charge it. Most plan to pay it off fast. 84% of 2026 summer travelers will use a credit card for at least some trip costs, and 61% say they'll clear the balance with the first statement. But 23% say they'll charge it and won't pay it off right away, according to NerdWallet's 2026 Summer Travel Report (Harris Poll). That's the group this issue is for.
02
Last summer's balances are still lingering. Of 2025 summer travelers who charged their trip, 74% didn't pay it off with the first statement, and 35% still haven't paid it off at all, months later. That debt didn't stay on vacation — it came home, and it's still compounding.
03
Interest is the real cost, not the swipe. The average card carrying a balance sits in the low-to-mid 22% APR range, per Federal Reserve G.19 Consumer Credit data. Charge a $3,000 trip and pay it down slowly at that rate, and you can add close to $700 to $1,000 in interest over a year. That's real money for a week that already ended.
