All-Inclusive Is Hiding These 5 Fees in 2026

Travel brochures print “all-inclusive” on the buffet photo. What they print smaller is what “all” doesn't cover. Here's the real add-on math for a Cancun trip.

By Chris Bacon  ·  Founder, Vacation Math  ·  7 min read

I spent last week pricing out Cancun all-inclusives for the Fourth of July. Found some hotels that looked legitimately cheap — until I kept digging and discovered the Visitax situation. It changed my plans entirely.

Travel brochures love the word “all-inclusive.” They print it on the photos of the buffet, the rooftop pool, the smiling staff with a clean towel. What they print smaller — usually after the asterisk — is what “all” doesn't cover.

Here's a quick, honest list of what stacks on top of a 2026 Cancun all-inclusive, gathered from official Quintana Roo tax documentation and what travelers are actually paying this summer.

Five fees the brochure doesn't show you

01
Visitax — $15/person, paid online, not on your hotel bill. Quintana Roo's state tourist tax has been on the books since 2021 as an honor system. In 2026 that changed: agents at Cancun International are now scanning Visitax QR codes at security checkpoints, and travelers without one are getting pulled aside to pay on the spot — minutes before a flight. Your resort doesn't collect it; they can't. Pay it at the official Visitax site before you fly, save the QR code, and walk through. For a family of 4: $60 total, paid once.
02
State + federal lodging taxes — about 22% on the room rate. In Quintana Roo: the federal VAT (IVA) runs 16%, and the state lodging tax (ISH) runs about 6%. These are usually baked into the all-inclusive package price — but always check. If a resort quotes “$200/night plus tax,” that's $244, not $200. Compare hotels on the final number, not the headline.
03
The environmental fee — $1–$4 per night, often cash at the desk. Small. Annoying. Not in your booking confirmation. For a 5-night Cancun stay, that's roughly $20 in pesos the front desk wants in small bills. Card-only travelers get to enjoy a side trip to the airport ATM.
04
“Premium” everything inside the resort. Specialty restaurants (often app-reservation-only, $20–$50 extra per person), top-shelf liquor, motorized water sports, the spa, and even Wi-Fi at some budget resorts sit outside the base package. What “all-inclusive” actually includes: the buffet, the house pour, and the daytime activities. Everything else is à la carte and priced like a hotel.
05
Airport transfers + tips. Not in the room rate. Plan roughly $18/person for a shared shuttle, or $60–$95 for a private van. Tips run about $10–$20/day if you want good service — yes, even at “all-inclusive” resorts, since service charges being technically included doesn't change how tipping actually works there.
