The Christmas All-Inclusive Premium Is Already Priced In

Holiday all-inclusive rates get set months ahead, and the booking window for Christmas week is closing now. Here is what the premium actually costs and the dates that dodge most of it.

By Chris Bacon  ·  Founder, Vacation Math  ·  6 min read

Holiday all-inclusive rates get set months ahead, and the booking window for Christmas week is closing now. Here is what the premium actually costs and the dates that dodge most of it.

This is a mid-September problem, which is why it is this week's issue. Holiday bookings for December 20 through 31 are generally made three to six months out. We are inside that window and moving toward the wrong end of it.

What the premium actually is

The holiday markup is not one number, it is three, and they step up hard across a ten-day span. Measured against November rates in Cancun:

Cancun hotel rates by December window
Premium over November
December 1 to 19+30 to 50%
December 20 to 26, Christmas week+80 to 150%
December 27 to 31, New Year's+100 to 200%

On all-inclusive pricing specifically, a standard property that runs $140 to $280 per person per night in early December moves to $280 to $600 for Christmas week. Luxury properties go from $350 to $700 up to $700 to $1,500. Four-star Hotel Zone all-inclusives sit at $350 to $450 a night over Christmas and New Year's, the highest they reach all year.

Punta Cana behaves the same way with slightly softer numbers. Holiday packages start around $280 a night at the larger Bavaro properties, and peak season flights run $450 to $700 round trip.

The two dates that save real money

Arriving December 21 or 22 instead of December 20 runs 15 to 20% cheaper for what is functionally the same trip. One or two days of shift, same Christmas, materially different bill.

The bigger one is on the other side. January 1 through 7 is genuinely cheap, and January overall runs 30 to 40% below December. If the trip is about warm weather and time together rather than the calendar date, moving it two weeks later is the single largest lever available. For a family of four at $400 a night, a week in mid-January instead of Christmas week is well over $1,000 back.

I understand that for a lot of families the date is the point. School is out, everyone is off, and January is not on the table. If that is you, the answer is not to move the trip, it is to know the number going in.

What people forget to add

The resort rate is the part everyone shops. The holiday premium applies to the things around it too. Rental cars over the holiday week run 40 to 60% above early December. Flights peak December 19 through 26. New Year's Eve dinners and parties at most resorts are ticketed separately, and NYE packages are frequently sold per stay with a three to five night minimum rather than per night, at $800 to $3,000 per person.

That last one catches people. A resort quoting an attractive nightly rate can require a five-night holiday minimum, which makes the real decision a $4,000 one, not a $400 one.

Four things to actually do this week

01
Decide this month if Christmas week is actually the trip. The three-to-six month window closes in September for December travel. Waiting until October narrows your resort list before it raises your price, and it does both.
02
Price December 21 or 22 against December 20. Same holiday, 15 to 20% less. This is the cheapest change on the list because it costs you nothing.
03
Run mid-January as a real alternative, once. Even if you already know the answer is no, see the number. January runs 30 to 40% below December, and a single comparison tells you what the date is costing you.
04
Check the minimum-stay rule before you fall for the nightly rate. Holiday bookings frequently carry three to five night minimums and separately ticketed New Year's Eve events. The nightly rate is not the offer.
