Flying home for Christmas: what four seats actually cost.

Hopper’s cheap-end Christmas fare is $452 a seat. Four seats are $1,808. The day you fly moves that by hundreds, and Halloween is Saturday.

By Chris Bacon  ·  Founder, Vacation Math  ·  8 min read

Christmas Day 2026 is a Friday, December 25. New Year’s Day 2027 is the next Friday, January 1. If four of you are flying home for that stretch, the fare on the screen needs a national number next to it before you can tell whether it is high.

I read the public sources on October 27, 2026. I did not pull a live fare for one city pair, so nothing below is a quote out of your airport. The anchor is Hopper’s 2026 Holiday Travel Index, published September 25 and still showing these figures when I reread the page today. Domestic Christmas round trips are averaging $452. That is $85 more than Hopper’s Christmas figure last year, a 23% increase, and Hopper calls Thanksgiving and Christmas airfares a 10-year high.

Four people who each need a seat, at $452, is $1,808. The same four seats at last year’s Hopper Christmas figure would have been $340 less. Treat $1,808 as the number to beat with the quote in front of you.

What the $452 actually counts

Hopper’s own methodology is narrower than “the average Christmas ticket.” The average is a demand-weighted average of the 10th percentile of prices available to book, using the previous one week of searches for holiday dates. It leans toward the cheaper fares people could actually buy that week, weighted toward the trips people were searching. An afternoon nonstop on December 23 can sit well above it.

Optional bags and seat assignments are outside that fare. A lap infant is a different ticket, and there is no single national price for one, so this sample is four seats. Thanksgiving in the same index is $402 a seat, or $1,608 for four. Christmas is the more expensive of the two holidays by $50 a seat, $200 for four.

The day you fly moves the bill

Hopper’s date notes are in that same September 25 index. The most expensive days to leave are the weekend ahead of Christmas, December 18, 19, and 20 (Friday, Saturday, and Sunday). Those fares are already averaging just below $500 a ticket. Four seats at a price just below $500 land just below $2,000.

The better departure days in that index are the Monday or Tuesday before the holiday. In 2026 that is December 21 and December 22. Fares there average around $440. Four seats: around $1,760 (4 × $440).

The gap between those two Hopper figures is on the order of $60 a seat, about $240 for four. Both numbers are rounded. On your route the gap can be wider, or almost nothing. The $452 national snapshot sits between them, which is what you’d expect from a cheap-end average of the whole holiday.

Coming home is its own choice. Hopper says skip a Sunday, December 27 return. That day runs about 20% higher than early-in-the-week returns. I left it as a percent. Hopper didn’t print the fare that 20% is added to, and the $440 figure above is a departure price, not a return price.

If the trip runs through New Year’s, Hopper says come back before January 2. On January 2, prices surge to over $560 a ticket as people head home for good. Four seats at $560 would be $2,240, so “over $560” is over $2,240. Set next to the around-$440 Monday or Tuesday departure, the gap is more than $120 a seat, more than $480 for four. Same index, still rounded, still not your airport. January 2, 2027 is a Saturday.

USA Today on October 2, citing Expedia’s Holiday Travel Outlook, points at a different cheap pattern: leave Christmas Eve, Thursday, December 24, and come back Monday, December 28. That story doesn’t attach a dollar to those two days. If your dates can move, price December 21 and 22 against December 18 to 20, and price a December 28 return against December 27 and January 2. Keep the lower total.

If home is abroad

USA Today’s October 2 story reports Hopper’s Christmas averages by region. Hopper’s own page confirms the direction for two of them, Europe up 15% and Mexico and Central America up 23%, and it doesn’t print these dollar amounts. One date lines up cleanly. December 22, Hopper’s cheaper Tuesday to leave, is 56 days from today. That is Google’s historical low point for a departure on that date. December 21 is 55 days out, one day past its own 56-day mark. December 18, the start of the expensive weekend, is 52 days out. Its 56-day mark was October 23, and it stays inside Google’s 33-to-67-day range through November 15.

January 2 is 67 days from today, the early edge of that same Google range. The 56-day mark for a January 2 flight is November 7. Being inside the booking window does not make January 2 a cheap day to fly. Hopper’s surge, over $560, is about the day, separate from the question of when you click buy.

Google also says the weekday you buy on barely matters. In the October 5 analysis, Wednesday beat Sunday by about 1.4%. The day you fly matters more. A Monday departure and a Tuesday or Wednesday return saves about 14% versus weekend trips, and up to 20% on domestic trips versus leaving and returning on Sunday. That 14% is Google’s general finding, across five years of U.S. departures. I did not knock 14% off the $452, because Hopper’s Christmas dates are the holiday-specific numbers.

Issue #13, from August 25, used an older reading: cheapest Christmas fares around 51 days out, with Halloween as the deadline. Google’s October update moves the low to 56 days and the range to 33 to 67. The practical reading this Tuesday matches the August advice in one way. You are in the window. Waiting until December is the part the sources agree gets more expensive. For a December 25 departure, Google’s low range runs through November 22, and the calculator’s Christmas window runs through November 19.

Leave the spring average out of it

The Bureau of Transportation Statistics said on September 29, 2026 that the average U.S. domestic airfare in the second quarter of 2026 was $445. In that release, round-trip itineraries were 55% of the sample and averaged $549. One-way itineraries were 45% and averaged $312. Those fares include the taxes and carrier charges required to board. Optional bags and seat fees are out.

Four of those second-quarter round trips, at $549, would be $2,196. That is what people paid in April, May, and June, across cheap trips and expensive ones. Hopper’s $452 is the cheaper end of Christmas fares that were bookable in one September week. Putting them side by side and calling Christmas “$97 cheaper than spring” mixes two different measurements. Use Hopper to compare a Christmas day with another Christmas day. Use your own quote to decide the trip.

What to do this week

01
Price the real route today. Four seats at $452 is $1,808, and that is a national cheap-end snapshot from searches before September 25. If the quote on your screen is near it, you are looking at the part of the market Hopper measured. If it is far above, change the dates before you change the trip.
02
Move the day before you move the airport. Test December 21 and 22 against December 18 to 20. Test a return on December 28, or another mid-week day before January 2, against December 27 and January 2. On Hopper’s rounded figures, the departure gap is about $240 for four, and the January 2 gap is more than $480 for four.
03
Put that quote on the booking curve. The When to Book calculator does not search live fares. Set the holiday to Christmas, type the price you are seeing, and it places that departure on a 36-to-72-day window. For a December 25 flight, the midpoint of that window is October 28, the day after this issue.
04
Add bags and seats from the airline, not from this page. Hopper and the government fare average both stop at the ticket. A checked bag for four people can matter as much as shifting a day. The airline’s fee page is the number. I don’t have one fee that fits every carrier, so I left it out.
