Fall Is Cheaper. Here's What It Actually Costs.

Cheaper than summer is not the same as cheap. Five realistic fall trips for a family of four, priced with current 2026 shoulder-season numbers.

By Chris Bacon  ·  Founder, Vacation Math  ·  8 min read

Summer's over. Kids are back in school. And every travel site is suddenly running the same story: fall is cheaper.

They're not wrong. Flights typically drop 25 to 40% after Labor Day, hotels and cabins follow, and crowds thin out enough that the weather beats what a lot of places had in July. Here's what they leave out: cheaper than summer is not the same as cheap. So I ran the actual numbers on five realistic fall trips for a family of four, using current 2026 shoulder-season pricing.

What still catches people

Food almost always runs higher than planned. There's always one more attraction or dinner that “doesn't count.” And gas, parking, and random fees rarely show up in the hotel search.

That gap between the sticker price and the real total is the same in every region above. Only the size of it changes.

Four ways to book smarter this fall

09
Book lodging with a kitchen. It quietly cuts the food line by hundreds over a week, in every region above.
10
Price the shoulder dates first. The week right after Labor Day or right before Thanksgiving often runs 20 to 30% cheaper than the weeks on either side of it.
11
Buy the annual park pass before single tickets. An $80 America the Beautiful Pass pays for itself after three national park stops in a year.
12
Set your number before you search. Let the destination follow the budget instead of the other way around.
