Six Cruise Lines Raised Gratuities This Year. Here Is Every Rate.

Most gratuity comparisons you will find online were written before the last two increases, so they are quoting numbers that no longer exist. Here is every major line's current rate and what it adds up to for a family.

By Chris Bacon  ·  Founder, Vacation Math  ·  6 min read

Most gratuity comparisons you will find online were written before the last two increases, so they are quoting numbers that no longer exist. Here is every major line's current rate and what it adds up to for a family.

Six lines raised their daily rate between February and July. That is not a trend, that is the whole industry moving at once.

Who raised rates and when

Margaritaville at Sea went first on February 1. Princess followed on March 8, Carnival on April 2, MSC on May 11, Holland America on June 1, and Celebrity on July 29. Royal Caribbean, Norwegian, Virgin Voyages, and Disney held their rates steady.

Celebrity's is the one worth flagging. It raised rates on July 29 and notified passengers the same day, which gave nobody a window to prepay at the old number. Carnival, by contrast, honored the old rate for anyone who had prepaid before April 2.

Suites run higher on every line. Carnival suites are $19, Princess full suites $20, Royal Caribbean around $21, Celebrity's Retreat $24.50, Norwegian's Haven $25, and Disney concierge $27.25. MSC's Yacht Club sits at $23.

The percentage nobody adds in

The daily rate is only half the system. There is a separate service charge on bar tabs, beverage packages, and specialty dining, and it moved this year too. Holland America took its charge from 18% to 20% on June 1, the same day the daily rate went up. Carnival and Princess also sit at 20%.

So a line can raise your cost twice in one announcement, and coverage tends to report only the dollar figure. If you drink onboard, the percentage is where the money actually leaks.

What a family of four actually pays

Seven nights, standard cabin, two adults and two kids. There is no child discount on gratuities on any mainstream line, and Norwegian charges from age three.

Add a modest bar tab and two specialty dinners with 20% on each, and most families clear $650 in service charges before a single excursion.

Notice the shape of this cost. It scales with people, not with cabins. A couple in the same stateroom pays half. This is the line item that quietly punishes family cruising, and it is almost never in the fare you compared.

Can you remove them

It depends on the line and it has been getting harder. Royal Caribbean and Carnival still allow adjustments at Guest Relations onboard. Norwegian will adjust for an unresolved service issue. Holland America calls its charge crew appreciation and Margaritaville at Sea does not allow removal at all.

I will say the quiet part. This money goes to the crew who cleaned your cabin and served your dinner, and removing it to save $500 is a decision about them, not about the cruise line. Budget for it as a fixed cost, the same way you budget for port fees, and the question stops coming up.

Four things to actually do this week

01
Add gratuities to the fare before you compare two cruises. A $200 fare difference disappears fast when one line charges $17 a day and the other charges $22. For a family of four across a week, that gap is $140.
02
Prepay if you are sailing more than a few months out. On most lines prepaying locks the current rate against an increase, which six lines' worth of passengers would have benefited from this year. On Virgin Voyages it is simply cheaper, $20 a night instead of $22.
03
Price the drink package against 20% on every round. The package includes its service charge. Paying per drink does not. If you are a two-drinks-a-day household, run both numbers rather than assuming the package is a splurge.
04
Watch for gratuities-included promotions in Wave Season. January through March, lines routinely bundle prepaid gratuities as a booking perk. On a family sailing that is worth $476 to $616, often more than the onboard credit offered next to it.
