Pick an arrondissement and a bakery

Paris rewards a neighborhood you can walk in slippers and a Metro you tap without thinking. The tower is a view — Trocadéro or Champ de Mars on Budget — not a lodging strategy. The 10th–11th is where dinner is honest. A Gare hotel is fine the night you land early; it is a sad week if you never move. Stairs are the elevator. Pack light.

Base yourself here

Sleep on a Metro line you will actually use. Visit the tower; do not pay to wake up under it.

  • Ibis or a walk-up in the 10th–11th or 18th–19th — bakery downstairs, Metro in five minutes.
  • Palace or palace-adjacent (Crillon, Bristol, Lutetia class) only if leftover is silly. Paris mid already eats a US city budget.

Three days that stay in walking distance

One museum day. Two walking days. Dinner on your block.

Day 1

Your arrondissement, then the river

Bakery crawl on the block. Île de la Cité / Left Bank walk. Eiffel from Trocadéro at dusk. Do not “do” the summit on night one, and do not eat on the steps.

Day 2

One timed museum

Louvre or Orsay — you already picked. Late afternoon in the Marais or along the Canal. Neighborhood dinner. If you try both museums, you will remember the cafeteria.

Day 3

A cemetery, a smaller room, or Versailles if leftover

Père Lachaise or a second smaller museum (not a third mega). Versailles only if leftover covers the half-day and you accept the RER. Evening: fromagerie, wine, stairs, bed.

February is still Paris. August is a tourist menu.

August is half-closed and fully priced for tourists. Shoulder spring is pretty and no longer cheap on weekends.

Go Late January through March, and November before the holiday lights become a room tax.

Skip unless that is the trip Late June through August, and the two weeks around Christmas and New Year’s.

February is the value window and still Paris. Book the Atlantic crossing 2–4 months out, midweek. Open-jaw (in Paris, out Rome) often beats two one-ways.

An arrondissement you can walk in slippers

Ibis or a walk-up on a Metro line in the 10th, 11th, 18th, or 19th. A tower-block view is a tourist tax.

Ibis or Hotel F1 on a Metro line, Generator or St. Christopher’s on the Canal or at Gare du Nord, or a 2-star walk-up near République or Oberkampf with a bakery downstairs. That is the trip: stairs, a carnet, and dinner on the block.

A Left Bank or Canal 3-star — Malte or Odeon-class, a Canal Saint-Martin boutique, Luxembourg or Bastille — if you want one neighborhood and a quieter stair. Crillon, Cheval Blanc, Ritz, or Bristol only if leftover is silly. Lutetia-class Left Bank if you must have a name and still want to walk.

Prix-fixe traps

The closer the monument, the worse the formule.

  • Restaurants on the tower, the hill, or the museum steps — A tourist menu with a view surcharge. Walk five minutes.
  • A four-museum day — One timed ticket. The rest is walking you will actually remember.
  • A Gare hotel for the whole week — Fine for an early arrival. Sad as a neighborhood.

Navigo. Stairs. Trocadéro for the tower.

  5. Eiffel from Trocadéro or Champ de Mars on Budget. The summit is a pick-one with Sainte-Chapelle.
  6. Book the Atlantic crossing 2–4 months out, midweek. Open-jaw (in Paris, out Rome) often beats two one-ways.
  7. Fromagerie + wine is a valid dinner. Palace-hotel dining only if leftover is silly.
