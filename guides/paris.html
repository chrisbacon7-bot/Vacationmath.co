Paris

Bakeries and one reserved dinner beat a week of tourist-menu prix fixes on the tower steps.

Quick facts

Best months

Who this is for

For

  • People who will pick an arrondissement they can walk in slippers and tap the Metro without thinking
  • Anyone who wants one timed museum and dinner on the block

Not for

  • Anyone who wants a rental car in Paris
  • People stacking four museums and a tower-steps formule in one day

Getting around

Navigo or a carnet. Stairs are the elevator. The tower is a Trocadéro view on Budget, not a summit default.

You usually don’t need a car.

Transit-first

  • Metro + RER. A Navigo week (if your days qualify) beats a fistful of t+ tickets
  • Walk one arrondissement in the morning. Pack light — stairs are common
  • CDG vs ORY: RER B is the honest CDG move. A taxi is Splurge, not Budget

Where to stay

Ibis or a walk-up on a Metro line in the 10th, 11th, 18th, or 19th. A tower-block view is a tourist tax.

Budget

  • Ibis Paris Gare du Nord points-friendly Metro downstairs, not a tower-block view
  • Ibis Styles Paris République points-friendly 10th/11th, bakery downstairs
  • Holiday Inn Express Paris – Canal de la Villette points-friendly Metro line, Budget if you land late
  • Generator Paris hostel-plus, Canal Saint-Martin

Mid-range

  • Hilton Paris Opera points-friendly 8th/9th, Metro in five minutes
  • Paris Marriott Opera Ambassador points-friendly Opera neighborhood, one arrondissement
  • Hyatt Regency Paris Étoile points-friendly 17th, Metro / RER, skip changing arrondissements nightly
  • Hôtel Malte Opéra or a 3-star near Luxembourg boutique if you want Left Bank walking

Splurge

  • Park Hyatt Paris-Vendôme points-friendly 1st Splurge
  • Four Seasons Hotel George V 8th Splurge

Where to eat

Bakery plus coffee on your block every morning. Skip anything on the tower, the hill, or the museum steps.

Budget

  • Breakfast bakery + coffee on your block (Du Pain et des Idées class if you are in the 10th)
  • Lunch Bouillon Chartier, Bouillon Pigalle, or a formule du midi
  • Dinner L’As du Fallafel or a neighborhood bistro, not the tower
  • L’As du Fallafel or a neighborhood bistro not the tower

Mid-range

  • Breakfast bakery most mornings
  • Lunch Bouillon Julien or a market street (Marché des Enfants Rouges)
  • Dinner one reserved bistro (Frenchie wine bar / a 10th–11th table)
  • Fromagerie + wine is a valid dinner

Splurge

  • Breakfast bakery, then one café sit-down
  • Lunch a proper bistro, not a museum cafeteria
  • Dinner reservation: Septime, Frenchie, or Le Comptoir book before you fly
  • Palace-hotel dining only if the budget still has room is silly

Things to do

Louvre or Orsay — pick one timed ticket. A four-museum day is how you buy souvenirs you will not remember.

Free / cheap

  • Île de la Cité + Left Bank walk Notre-Dame exterior, Seine (free)
  • Père Lachaise or Canal Saint-Martin walk (free)
  • Eiffel Tower from Trocadéro or Champ de Mars, not the summit on Budget (free)
  • Eiffel from Trocadéro or Champ de Mars, not the summit on Budget (free)

Paid — pick one

  • Louvre or Orsay pick one timed ticket (ticketed)
  • Sainte-Chapelle or a tower summit pick one (ticketed)

3-day skeleton

Day 1

Your arrondissement, then the river

  • Bakery crawl on the block
  • Île de la Cité / Left Bank walk
  • Eiffel from Trocadéro at dusk — do not eat on the steps

Day 2

One timed museum

  • Louvre or Orsay — you already picked
  • Late afternoon in the Marais or along the Canal
  • Neighborhood dinner

Day 3

A cemetery, a smaller room, or Versailles if you already priced it

  • Père Lachaise or a second smaller museum (not a third mega)
  • Versailles only if you accept the RER half-day
  • Evening: fromagerie, wine, stairs, bed

Book before you go

  • Louvre or Orsay timed ticket — pick one
  • One 10th–11th dinner if you want Septime / Frenchie / Le Comptoir
  • Navigo if your days qualify, otherwise a carnet
  • Atlantic crossing 2–4 months out, midweek. Open-jaw (in Paris, out Rome) often beats two one-ways

Hidden costs

  • VAT in the room rate — still not a US-style surprise, but mid already eats a US city budget
  • Taxis from CDG vs RER B
  • Tower summit + Sainte-Chapelle stacked as “both”
  • Museum-steps formules
  • Bags on stairs if you packed like a palace

Skip this

  • Restaurants on the tower, the hill, or the museum steps — A tourist menu with a view surcharge. Walk five minutes.
  • A four-museum day — One timed ticket. The rest is walking you will actually remember.
  • A Gare hotel for the whole week — Fine for an early arrival. Sad as a neighborhood.

Keep the number honest

Top money-saving tips

  1. Bakery breakfast on your block. Du Pain et des Idées class if you slept in the 10th — not the hotel buffet.
  2. Bouillon Chartier / Pigalle or a formule du midi. Skip restaurants on the tower, the hill, and the museum steps.
  3. Navigo or a carnet. Taxis are Splurge. Pack light — stairs are common.
  4. Louvre or Orsay — pick one timed ticket. A four-museum day is how you buy souvenirs you will not remember.
  5. Eiffel from Trocadéro or Champ de Mars on Budget. The summit is a pick-one with Sainte-Chapelle.
  6. Book the Atlantic crossing 2–4 months out, midweek. Open-jaw (in Paris, out Rome) often beats two one-ways.
  7. Fromagerie + wine is a valid dinner. Palace-hotel dining only if you already priced it.
  8. 10th–11th or 18th–19th on a Metro line. A tower-block view is a tourist tax.
