Who this is for

For

  • People who will tap a bank card, hit the daily cap, and sleep in Zone 1–2
  • Anyone who wants free museums and one West End seat

Not for

  • Anyone who wants a rental car (Congestion Charge on purpose)
  • People stacking the Eye and the Tower on Budget

Getting around

Contactless. Never a car. Zone 1–2 lodging.

You usually don’t need a car.

Transit-first

  • Contactless daily cap on Tube / bus. A Visitor Oyster only if you like souvenirs
  • South Bank, Bloomsbury, or South Ken — one neighborhood, no car
  • LHR via Elizabeth Line or Piccadilly. LGW and STN are different ground math — price it

Where to stay

Premier Inn or Travelodge in Southwark, King’s Cross, or Earl’s Court. The Hoxton if you want restaurants on the block. Claridge’s or The Connaught if Mayfair is the point.

Budget

  • Premier Inn London County Hall South Bank walk, not a West End rate
  • Ibis London Euston or Holiday Inn Express Southwark Zone 1–2, Tesco nearby
  • Travelodge Covent Garden compact, if you packed light

Mid-range

  • The Hoxton restaurants on the block
  • Hilton Bankside or London Marriott County Hall South Bank, walk to a station
  • Canopy by Hilton London City Aldgate, not Mayfair

Splurge

  • Claridge's or The Connaught Mayfair is the point
  • The Savoy the river Splurge
  • The Langham or Raffles at The OWO one of them, not a week of landmark tickets as well

Where to eat

Bakery or a Tesco meal deal. Borough Market for one lunch. Dishoom or a Dalston grill for dinner. The Ivy is the reservation, then Tesco again.

Budget

  • A bakery, or a Tesco meal deal when you are between museums
  • Borough Market or Maltby Street one lunch
  • A pub pie or a Zone 2 grill dinner

Mid-range

  • Dishoom one dinner
  • Barrafina if that is the booking
  • A neighborhood Indian the nights you are not in the West End

Splurge

  • The Ivy the reservation, then Tesco again
  • Core or Kitchen Table one tasting
  • A hotel breakfast only if it is already in the Premier Inn or Travelodge rate

Things to do

The British Museum and the National Gallery are free. Pay for one West End seat or the Tower, not the Eye and the Tower the same day.

Budget

  • British Museum or the National Gallery free
  • South Bank walk, Tate Modern turbine hall free
  • Columbia Road or a market morning

Mid-range

  • Contactless daily cap on Tube and bus
  • Elizabeth line from Heathrow if your hotel is on that line
  • One of the Tower, the Eye, or a West End day seat

Splurge

  • A full-price orchestra seat the Splurge show
  • Windsor or Bath as its own day, not a third landmark
  • A Heathrow hotel for the week means you ride in every morning

3-day skeleton

Day 1

A free museum

  • British Museum or the National Gallery
  • Bakery or Tesco breakfast — hotel breakfast only if it is in the rate
  • South Bank walk. No Eye ticket

Day 2

One West End seat

  • TodayTix or a box-office day seat
  • Dishoom or Barrafina if that is the dinner
  • Not the Tower the same day

Day 3

The other neighborhood

  • South Ken museums if you slept nearby, still free where they are free
  • Or the Tower if you did not buy a show
  • Elizabeth Line home. Not a Heathrow hotel for the week

Book before you go

  • West End rush / day seats if that is the night
  • Contactless — you almost never need a paper ticket
  • Atlantic crossing 2–4 months out, midweek
  • Skip a rental. Congestion Charge is real

Hidden costs

  • Heathrow Express versus the Elizabeth Line — different fares, same airport
  • A Visitor Oyster you did not need because contactless caps
  • The Eye and the Tower on one day
  • A full-price West End seat when a day seat existed
  • UK room quotes usually already include VAT. Do not add a second VAT, and do not treat Trip Plan’s 10% Europe assumption as the London rate

Skip this

  • A paper Travelcard by default — Contactless capping is the fare.
  • The Eye and the Tower together — The South Bank walk is the free one. Pick one paid landmark.
  • A Heathrow hotel for the week — It is a landing night.

Cap the Tube, skip the paper ticket

Top money-saving tips

  1. Hidden gem: Price the Elizabeth line against Heathrow Express before you keep an airport hotel all week. Walk-up Express is the expensive one. Advance Express can undercut the Elizabeth line. The Piccadilly line is the slow cheap one. Tapping a contactless card on Express does not turn it into a Tube fare.
  2. Hidden gem: The British Museum and the National Gallery do not charge admission. The Eye and the Tower are the paid pair. Buy one.
  3. Hidden gem: Contactless capping is the fare. A Visitor Oyster and a paper Travelcard are souvenirs unless you have already counted the rides and the cap loses.
  4. Borough Market is one lunch. A Tesco meal deal covers the other two.
  5. UK hotel quotes usually include VAT. Compare two rates only when both are taxes-in. Trip Plan's 10% Europe assumption is not a London VAT quote.
